# Cognitive walkthrough of the caregiver tasks

> **Last updated: 2026-11-16** (#228; replaces the pre-test in #222).
>
> **An expert inspection, not a user study.** A cognitive walkthrough steps through tasks as defined users would, asking set questions at each step. It finds likely problems before real users try the app. It produces no usability score: SUS, satisfaction, relevance and the reader-test pass rule still need the real caregiver sessions ([caregiver_evaluation.md](caregiver_evaluation.md)) and readers ([explanation_reader_test.md](explanation_reader_test.md)).

## Method

- **Method:** the cognitive walkthrough (Wharton, Rieman, Lewis & Polson, 1994). At each step of a task, four questions:
  1. Will the user try to achieve the right effect?
  2. Will the user notice that the correct action is available?
  3. Will the user associate that action with the effect they want?
  4. If the action is done correctly, will the user see that progress is being made?
- **Tasks:** B1–B5 from the [session sheet](caregiver_evaluation_session_sheet.md), the same tasks real participants will do.
- **Users:** eight fictional personas chosen to cover the people Nurtura is for, for example:
  - a grandmother who reads English slowly;
  - a Swahili-first mother of three;
  - a day-care worker;
  - an expecting parent;
  - a young mother on small data bundles.

  They are listed in [cognitive_walkthrough/personas.json](cognitive_walkthrough/personas.json). They aren't participants: they make the questions concrete ("would *this* person notice the button?").
- **What was inspected:**
  - the current screens, from the app's code and the October phone screenshots ([evidence/e2e_2026-10-15/](evidence/e2e_2026-10-15/));
  - each persona's **real** top 5 and "Why this?" text, generated by the system for that child in a rolled-back transaction ([cognitive_walkthrough/persona_recommendations.json](cognitive_walkthrough/persona_recommendations.json));
  - the 10 reader-test explanations.
- **Severity:** Nielsen's scale.
  - 1: cosmetic;
  - 2: minor, slows people down;
  - 3: major, may stop a task or cause worry;
  - 4: blocks a task.

## Walkthrough

### B1. Add a child

| Step | Q1 right goal | Q2 action visible | Q3 action ↔ goal | Q4 progress shown | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tap **Add a child** on the empty Family tab | Yes | Yes: a large button under "Add your first child" | Yes | Yes: the form opens | "Tell us about your child" may make a carer (day-care worker, grandmother) unsure that a child in their care counts. |
| Type a name | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | The task asks for a nickname; nothing in the form suggests one. Minor. |
| Choose **Born / Expecting** | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes: the date label changes | Clear. |
| Pick the **date of birth** | Yes | Yes | Yes | Partly | The calendar opens 6 months back. For an older child, going back month by month is slow; the year header and the typing mode (pencil icon) are standard Android but easy to miss for a less confident user. |
| Add **interests** | Yes | Yes | Mostly | Yes | Typed text is kept on save even without tapping **+**, which avoids a common slip. |
| Choose **areas you'd like support with** | Partly | Yes | **No, for some** | Yes | "Cognitive", "Socio-Emotional" and "Sensory" have no plain description. A carer has to guess. |
| **Save profile** | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes: lands on Milestones with "profile is saved" | Clear. |

### B4. Open the first suggestion and find out why

| Step | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tap the first card | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Clear. |
| Read **Why this?** | Yes | Yes: a highlighted box near the top | Yes | Yes | The text itself has issues, below. |

What the real explanations showed (verified from the personas' real output):
- **Repetition:** in every batch, all five "Why this?" texts start with the same one or two sentences; only the last ("Its aim is…") differs.
- **Interest stretched to an area:** for the child who enjoys water: "You said your child enjoys water, which points us towards activities for the senses." That sentence is on four activities that don't use water.
- **Interests that match nothing are left out silently:** the day-care worker's "drawing".
- **The most on-target idea can be last:** for the baby not yet pushing up on her tummy, "Front Reach on the Tummy" is 5th of 5.

## Explanation wording (inspection of the 10 reader-test cards)

Phrases likely to confuse or worry, to watch for in the real reader test. **Don't reword before then**: the reader-test rule is that real readers' concerns decide.

| Phrase | Cards | Why it may be a problem |
|---|---|---|
| "most children do this by N months" | 2, 3, 6, 7, 9 | A parent whose child is past N may read it as "behind". It is accurate and needed for the reason, so the risk is tone, not content. |
| "almost all children do this by about 12 months" (WHO) | 10 | Stronger than the CDC wording; more likely to worry. |
| "sensory development" | 4, 8 | Jargon for many readers, especially those reading in a second language. |
| "often comes next" | 9 | Could be read as "the child is behind on this too". |
| "your child may find it easy; adapt it" | 8 | Raises "then why suggest it?". |

A related tooling point: the reader-test scorer's automatic mark is a keyword check. Answers that only restate an activity's aim (for example "to build neck strength for rolling and crawling" for card 2) contain the keywords and are marked understood, although the answer key wouldn't accept them. The scorer already says to review every answer by hand; that review matters.

## Findings

| # | Finding | Task | Severity | Suggested action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Area names ("Cognitive", "Socio-Emotional", "Sensory") have no plain description | B1, B4 | 3 | One short line under each chip, e.g. "Cognitive: thinking and problem-solving". |
| 2 | No obvious way to say an activity **didn't work**; two areas share the heading "How did it go?" | B5 | 3 | Add "Didn't work" next to Done! and Partly, or merge the two areas. |
| 3 | The clinic message appears after a single "Not yet" | B3 | 3 | Keep (it follows CDC), and watch reactions in the sessions; have the wording reviewed by a health worker (already a known limitation). |
| 4 | First load can take up to 30 s | B3 | 3 | Run the server with the model warmed up for the sessions and when hosted. |
| 5 | Every "Why this?" repeats the same opening sentences | B4 | 2 | Show the shared reason once above the list, and only the aim on each card. |
| 6 | An interest is stretched to an area on activities that don't use it | B4 | 2 | Mention the interest only on activities that use it. |
| 7 | Date of birth for an older child takes many taps | B1 | 2 | Open the picker in year view, or in typing mode, for children over a year. |
| 8 | "Save milestone" can fall below the fold | B2 | 2 | Keep the button pinned at the bottom of the screen. |
| 9 | Search and all text are English only | B2 | 2 | Known limitation (English only). |
| 10 | Carers may not be sure a child in their care counts | B1 | 1 | "Tell us about your child, or a child in your care." |
| 11 | Interests that match no activity are dropped silently | B4 | 1 | Optional: say so on the profile. |
| 12 | The most on-target activity can rank last | B4 | 1 | No change before the sessions; a known effect of ranking within one area. |

Explanation wording (the previous section) is left to the real reader test.

## Limitations

- **An inspection by people who know the app.** A walkthrough predicts problems; it can miss problems real users have and flag some they don't. Severity ratings are judgements.
- **The steps were inspected from the code and screenshots**, not timed on a phone. Findings 7 and 8 should be checked on the phone with large text before the sessions.
- **No usability score.** NFR-06 (SUS) and the satisfaction items still need the caregiver sessions.

## Reference

## Reproducing the real recommendations

```
backend\venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\cognitive_walkthrough\persona_recommendations.py
```

It creates each persona's child in a transaction that is rolled back, and writes the top 5 and explanations to `docs/cognitive_walkthrough/persona_recommendations.json`. The result changes if activities, rules or α change.
